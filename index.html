GIRA Bike-Share — Where the Network Fails

Group submission, 4 people · cleaning, analysis, report and deck are mine

Four years of Lisbon bike-share availability for EMEL, turned into a ranked list of which station needs a van and at what hour. Eight source files that don't share a schema: 2020 logs empty docks and no status, 2022 onwards logs total docks plus status, and 2021 carries both, which is what makes the harmonisation testable rather than assumed. Dropped 3.4M byte-identical rows the logger had rewritten, flagged the impossible readings and the maintenance rows instead of deleting them, and kept maintenance out of every denominator so a station out of service isn't counted as one that worked.

The network is empty in 5.24% of snapshots, but that average hides station-hours above 30%. Station 519 at 09:00 is empty a third of the time and tops the priority list, and pairing it with a station that fills up in the same hour gives a van loop of 1.07 km.

Elevation explains the direction of failure at 0.386, against 0.061 for latitude. Riders coast downhill and don't ride back up, so hillside stations drain and riverside ones overflow.

AdventureWorks Profitability Audit

Started from a paradox: revenue growing, profit per transaction falling. Worked it through three angles, seasonality, structural trend and pricing policy, and the answer was the discounts. Revenue of €109.8M against €9.37M profit, with the 2013 discounted segment alone bleeding close to €1M.

One view (vw_Grupo2) joining order headers, details and product tables, filtered to completed orders so every number downstream agrees. Eight integrity tests before any analysis, then window functions for the rankings, LAG for year-over-year growth, and the elasticity query behind the finding below.

Discounts past 10% kill volume instead of buying it. Roughly €1M of lost profit traces back to a pricing policy, not to demand.

NYC Taxi Analysis

Two dashboards off the same star-schema model, one for the vendor and one for the driver, because they want opposite things. Cleaned the raw trip data, derived pickup hour and weekday, classified vehicles by passenger count, then built the DAX for tip-per-mile and income-per-trip.

The deck walks through it one business question at a time, each slide ending on what to actually do about it. Demand sits on a 13:00–18:00 plateau and Manhattan and Boro zones carry 98% of trips.

21% of revenue traces back to an unknown vendor. That needs fixing before anyone trusts the revenue numbers on either dashboard.

Titanic Survival Analysis

Cleaned the data first: dropped Cabin at 77% missing, imputed Age with medians split by gender since the distributions differ, filled Embarked with the mode. Ran chi-square tests to confirm Sex and Pclass were actually signal, then trained logistic regression, a decision tree and a random forest on an 80/20 split. Random forest took it on F1, at 0.741.

Three research questions in the deck, each one going past the obvious answer. Being a child nearly tripled a boy's odds and did almost nothing for a girl's, and travelling in a family of 2 to 4 beat travelling alone by a wide margin.

Gender dominates, but class moves it. First-class women survived at 96.8%, third-class women at 50%.
